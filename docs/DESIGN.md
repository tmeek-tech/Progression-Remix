Forge 1.20.1 overhaul mod (mod id `mcremix`). Hard dependencies: Create 6.0.x and, since 0.14.0, Create: Power Grid 0.6.2 (with Architectury API, which it needs). Soft integration: Immersive Weathering. Designed to sit alongside William Wythers' Overhauled Overworld (WWOO) and Better Combat.

## Guiding rules (from the brief)

1. **Remix, don't invent.** Every system grows out of something vanilla or Create already has: copper weathering, campfires, bricks, anvils, zombie villagers, Create heat levels.
2. **Families of similar things.** If you know how one member behaves, you can guess the rest (copper shaft → copper cog → waxed copper cog).
3. **Everything earns its place.** Most blocks have a job. Some can be mostly decorative (Taran, 2026-10-20), but then they should feel real and grounded, the way Immersive Weathering's do, not be standard blocks in new textures; wooden parts in every wood are that kind.
4. **Sidegrades, not upgrades.** Each material has a personality with a real drawback; early materials keep a niche forever.
5. **Show, don't tell.** Particles, sounds and animation communicate state; Create Ponder scenes explain every new interaction.

## The shared vocabulary

### Heat (one scale for everything that cooks, fires or smelts)

| Level | Name | Sources (the block directly below, or the device's own fuel) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cold | nothing; unlit campfires |
| 1 | Warm | campfire, magma block, wood fuel (logs, planks, sticks), a blaze burner with a resting blaze |
| 2 | Hot | fire, lava, charcoal / coal / coal block / lava bucket fuel, Immersive Weathering's charred wood |
| 3 | Searing | Coal Coke or blaze rod fuel, soul fire, soul campfire, a fed blaze burner |
| 4 | Blazing | a superheated blaze burner (Blaze Cake); hydrogen or ethylene pumped fast into a Steel Furnace (0.16.0) |

Power Grid's heaters (heating coil, basin heater) count by their real temperature: 200 °C is Warm, 400 °C Hot, 600 °C Searing, 1200 °C Blazing, the same thresholds Power Grid uses for its own fan processing and basin heating. They are in `mcremix:heat_sources/electric`, read through Power Grid's "thermal" behaviour at runtime (no compile dependency). Sources live in block tags (`mcremix:heat_sources/*`) and fuels in item tags (`mcremix:kiln_fuel/*`). A device uses whichever is hotter, the block below or its fuel. A running Bellows adds one level on top, up to Searing (it fans fires, not electric heaters: moving air only cools those).

**Lighting (Taran, 0.16.0).** Fuel in a furnace's firebox does not catch by itself: flint and steel or a fire charge lights it, by hand or from a Deployer, and so does a Spark Plug (below). Once lit it burns its fuel whether or not there is anything to fire, and whether or not that fuel is hotter than the heat from below, until the fuel runs out and the fire dies; then it has to be lit again. A shovel puts it out, by hand or from a Deployer, and leaves the fuel in, part-burnt or not, for next time. Heat from the block underneath needs no lighting. The block state shows whether the furnace's own fire is lit, and goggles say when there is fuel waiting to be lit.

Visual language: the furnace swaps to a lit model and glows brighter per level (light 9 / 12 / 14 / 15). Small flames lick out of the firebox at Warm, full flames at Hot and Searing, blue soul flames at Blazing; Searing and up spit lava sparks; chimney smoke thickens with heat. Each item in the chamber shows its own state: smoke while firing, drifting ash when the furnace is too cold for it. Engineer's Goggles show the exact level, fuel left, and what each slot is waiting for.

Recipes declare a **minimum heat**; every level above the minimum halves the time, and a recipe can name a bonus heat that doubles its output. A low-heat device is still the right tool for low-heat jobs: wood is cheap and plentiful, and a campfire-heated furnace never needs fuel at all.

**Steam boilers.** A furnace heats a Create boiler standing on it, the way a blaze burner does, and its own fire maps onto Create's boiler heat:

| Furnace's own fire (its fuel, plus a Bellows) | Boiler heat | The same as |
|---|---|---|
| none | whatever the block under the furnace would give the boiler | (passes straight through) |
| Warm or Hot | passive: the engine turns slowly and weakly | a campfire under the boiler |
| Searing (Coal Coke, blaze rods, or charcoal or coal fanned by a Bellows) | 1 | a fed blaze burner |

The boiler gets whichever is more, the furnace's own fire or the heat from underneath, and heat from underneath is never turned up: a soul campfire under a furnace is Searing for the chamber but still only simmers the boiler, and a superheated blaze burner under a furnace still superheats it. No solid fuel burns Blazing and a Bellows stops at Searing, so a Furnace on its own never goes past one heat level; a Steel Furnace burning Blazing (0.16.0) boils it as a superheated blaze burner does, two levels. Once lit, a furnace burns its fuel whether or not the boiler has an engine or whistle to drive (Taran, 0.16.0; before that it burned only for a working boiler), and fires its chamber meanwhile (fanned fuel still burns twice as fast). A tank, or any block resting where the chimney stands, caps the chimney: it disappears and the smoke vents out of the back (no soot either); goggles on the furnace say how hard it is boiling the boiler and, below one level, what would boil it. Why it matters: a furnace is a steam engine heater that needs no trip to the Nether, so steam power arrives with Coal Coke instead of with blazes. Blaze burners stay the stronger heater until the Steel Furnace, since they burn any fuel and, until then, only they superheat.

**Basins.** A Create basin standing on a furnace is heated the same way, for the mixer or press over it. The furnace's own fire counts as a blaze burner's heat: Warm or Hot only warms the basin, as a campfire under it would, and no heated recipe accepts that; Searing heats it like a fed blaze burner, enough for heated mixing and compacting such as brass. As with boilers, the basin gets whichever is more, the furnace's fire or what the block under the furnace would give a basin, so a soul campfire under a furnace still only warms the basin and a superheated blaze burner under it still superheats. Once lit, it burns its fuel whether or not the basin holds anything (0.16.0); when the heat changes it tells the basin, so a waiting mixer or press starts at once. While it heats a basin, small flames lick up around the basin's foot (blue when superheated); goggles on the furnace say how it heats the basin and, when that is not enough for heated recipes, what would. The basin's foot caps the chimney as a tank does. Why it matters: brass, the heart of Create's midgame, arrives with Coal Coke instead of a trip to the Nether for blazes. Superheated recipes still need a blaze burner fed Blaze Cake, under the basin or under the furnace, or a Steel Furnace burning Blazing.

### Materials (kinetic components)

Shafts, cogwheels, large cogwheels, brackets, gearboxes, clutches, gearshifts, chain drives and chain gearshifts all come in these materials (but brass, which since 0.23.0 has no clutch, gearshift or chain gearshift: Taran), and every part of one material shares its limits. Since 0.15.0 they turn in **supports** (next section). Since 0.27.0 (Taran) a material gives each kind of thing one limit: a support (a bracket, casing, gearbox, clutch, gearshift, chain drive or machine) its **Stability**, the top speed it turns at, and a shaft or cog its **Strength**, the load it carries. A shaft or cog turns as fast as the supports holding it allow, whatever it is made of, and supports never give way under load.

**Over the limit.** A support spun past its Stability grinds for about three seconds (smoke and a creaking knock for wood, sparks and a grinding squeal for metal, getting louder, cracks spreading over it), then gives way: a gearbox, clutch, gearshift, chain drive or machine breaks and drops, a bracket comes off and drops, and a casing is lost, leaving its part, which stays only if something else holds it. A shaft or cog in a network drawing more than its Strength, bare, bracketed or encased, wobbles in its seat for about a second (it spins on a tilted axis and sheds bits of itself), then breaks and drops as an item. Parts give way at slightly different moments, so the first break can relieve the rest of a line. Weathering never changes a top speed or a load limit. Materials live in the block tags `mcremix:kinetic_material/*` (Create's blocks and the pumps; this mod's parts know their own), so packs can retune; goggles on a part show its material, which support holds it or how far it reaches from one, the friction it draws, and its limits: "Stability: up to 256 RPM" on a support and on a part in a bracket or casing (the holder's), and "Strength: up to 4,096 su" (or "takes any load") on a shaft or cog.

**Stability and Strength (0.23.0, reworked in 0.27.0).** Every kinetic block made of a material, Create's included, has one more line in its tooltip, under Create's stress lines and drawn as Create draws them, a bar and a word; with goggles on it gives the RPM or su. Since 0.27.0 (Taran) a support's line is its **Stability**, its top speed: Wobbly for wood's 64 RPM, Shaky for brass's 96, Steady for andesite's 128, Stable for copper's and iron's 256, Very Stable for steel's and aluminium's 512, and Rock-solid for titanium's 2,048 (Taran's card). Gearboxes, clutches, gearshifts, chain drives, brackets, machines and casings show it, and so does Create's crushing wheel, which holds no shafts but keeps andesite's top speed. A shaft or cog's line is its **Strength**, the load it carries: Flimsy for wood's 2,048 su, Delicate for brass's 3,072, Light-duty for copper's 4,096, Sturdy for aluminium's 8,192, and Unbreakable for andesite, iron, steel and titanium, which carry any load. From 0.23.0 to 0.26.0 every part had two lines, Stability then meaning the load it carried and Max Speed its top speed at a support (Sluggish to Ludicrous).

### Supports (0.15.0)

Taran's redesign of Create's kinetics: shafts and cogs no longer hang in the air or carry any speed on their own; they turn in supports, the bearings of a machine. Until 0.23.0 they were called friction points; Taran renamed them, and the code keeps the old name (`FrictionPoints`, the config's `frictionPoints`). Heat only matters for aluminium and titanium, so it came last (0.18.0, under Heat and cooling).

- **What is one.** Machines that do work, pumps, gearboxes, clutches, gearshifts, chain drives and chain gearshifts, encased shafts and cogs, and a shaft or cog with a bracket bolted to a solid block. Sources (water wheels, windmills, engines, motors, cranks; a mechanical bearing only as a windmill), belts, gauges and Create's flywheel pass rotation on but hold nothing (`mcremix:supports`, which takes in the old `mcremix:friction_points`, and `mcremix:holds_no_shafts`). Since 0.23.0 a rotation speed controller holds the large cog in its top slot (Taran), as a bolted bracket would, and holds whatever that cog holds along its axis; the cog stays a bare part with no top speed of its own, so the controller can still drive it past brass's 96 RPM, though it keeps its Strength.
- **Holding.** A bare shaft or cog must be held: a support that takes a shaft on its side must be in line with it along its axis, within its material's reach, counting through the bare parts between them like scaffolding reaching out from a post (since 0.27.0, twice what they were: wood and brass 2 blocks, andesite, copper and iron 4, steel, aluminium and titanium 6). A part out of reach will not go on, like a torch with nothing to stand on (an action bar line says why), and one whose holder goes drops off as an item. Create's own placement helper and wrench obey the same rule, and parts at the edge of loaded chunks count as held so lines do not fall apart as chunks unload.
- **Brackets.** Every material has a bracket (each wood, brass, Create's metal bracket as andesite, copper, iron, steel and aluminium; Create's wooden bracket counts as wood). A bracket goes down on its own against a solid face (Taran, 0.15.1), its shaft lined up with a part pointing at it or else running the way you look (a wrench turns it a quarter turn on its plate), and a shaft or cog placed into it lies along it and takes it; breaking that part drops both, as before. Used on a shaft or cog already placed, a bracket's foot goes on the side away from you and bolts to the block there. Either way it holds only while that block has a solid face (a floor, wall, ceiling or the top of a post), so it will not go on with nothing to bolt to and falls off when its block goes. Holding brackets in the offhand fits one to each part as it is placed. Copper, iron and steel brackets weather like the parts, and honeycomb and an axe work on a part and its bracket together.
- **Casings.** Any shaft or cog, of any material, goes into any material's casing: andesite, brass, copper, iron (Iron Casing since 0.23.0, an iron sheet on a stripped log; Industrial Iron before), steel, aluminium and titanium. Using the casing on the part encases it (the casing is not used up, as in Create), a wrench while sneaking takes it off again, and breaking it drops the part inside. Since 0.26.0 (Taran) it works the other way round too: a cog or large cog used on a placed casing goes into it, the casing becoming its encased cog turning across the face that was clicked, and the casing pops back out, since Create never uses one up; sneaking places the cog beside the casing instead, and a deployer holding a cog encases it as a player does. Create's own andesite shaft and cogs keep Create's encased blocks in andesite and brass casing; everything else goes into this mod's encased shaft, cog and large cog for each casing, which draw the part inside in its own material. A part in a casing is out of the weather and keeps the stage it went in at. Parts encased in Industrial Iron before 0.23.0 keep working, and give back an Iron Casing when a wrench takes it off.
- **Top speed.** Only supports have one, their Stability. Since 0.27.0 (Taran) a bracketed or encased part takes its bracket's or casing's, whatever the part is made of (until then it took the lower of its part's and its holder's), and a gearbox, clutch, gearshift, chain drive or machine its own material's. Past it, the support grinds and gives way: a bracket drops, a casing is lost and leaves the part, which stays only if something else holds it. Bare parts between supports take any speed.
- **Load.** Only shafts and cogs carry a limited load, their Strength, bare, bracketed or encased (a load on a network counts against every one in it). Since 0.27.0 (Taran's card) supports never give way under load; until then brackets, casings, gearboxes, clutches, gearshifts, chain drives and machines carried only their material's load too.
- **Friction.** Only supports draw friction from the network: iron and steel ones are heavier to turn, and weathered copper, iron and steel ones add a little more. Bare parts draw none, however rusty.

Copper, iron and steel each get 8 variants per part (fresh / exposed / weathered / oxidized or rusted, plus waxed), mirroring vanilla copper so players instantly understand them. Iron and steel use Immersive Weathering's rust names. Aluminium never weathers, so it has one of each part.

**Every wood.** Wooden shafts, brackets, gearboxes, clutches and gearshifts come in all eleven woods (oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia, dark oak, mangrove, cherry, bamboo, crimson, warped), so wooden machinery can match what it is built into. Only the look changes: every wood has wood's limits, and each part sounds like its wood and has its log's map colour. Crimson and warped parts are cut from stems and, like the stems, do not burn (Taran's call). Each part is made of one wood: two of its logs make six of its shafts, four of its shafts round its stripped log (or stripped wood) make its gearbox, its stripped log, its shaft and redstone make its clutch, and its gearbox and redstone make its gearshift. A Bellows takes any wood's shaft (`mcremix:wooden_shafts`). The one wooden part of earlier versions (`wooden_shaft` and so on) loads as oak, which looks the same (`OldWoodenParts`).

**Wooden movers (0.17.0).** Mechanical bearings, mechanical and sticky pistons with their extension poles, gantry carriages and gantry shafts, and rope pulleys come in every wood too, for drawbridges and gates. Each is Create's mover in that wood and works as Create's does: a wooden piston pushes out its own wood's head on any wood's poles, slime makes it its wood's sticky piston, and a wooden carriage rides any gantry shaft, Create's included. Those that turn are wooden supports (64 RPM, 2,048 su); Create's own count as andesite (128 RPM, Taran). Recipes are Create's with that wood's stripped log (or stripped wood) as the casing and its shaft as the shaft (Taran's cards): the rope pulley takes its slab where Create's takes an iron sheet, the gantry carriage its gearbox where Create's takes a cog, poles are its planks round a stick (8) and gantry shafts its planks round redstone (8), so every wooden mover comes before andesite alloy. The rope and magnet stay Create's.

**Steel.** Steel is iron that has soaked up carbon, made the way it was in cementation furnaces. Four iron ingots, four Coal Coke and a clay ball to seal them make a **Cementation Pack** (shapeless). Fired at Searing, from any heat source, a pack comes out as four **Blister Steel** after 800 ticks, the slowest firing there is (Taran chose a crafted pack over packing iron in a coke fire or beside coke in the chamber). Blister steel is brittle, its skin raised in blisters, and crafts into nothing: only a Mechanical Press beats it into a **steel ingot**. Ingots press into **steel sheets**; a sheet applied to a stripped log makes **Steel Casing**, the casing for steel gearboxes, clutches, gearshifts and chain drives, as Create makes its casings. Steel is also under the common tags (`forge:ingots/steel`, `forge:nuggets/steel`, `forge:plates/steel`). Since 0.22.0 steel is a dark gunmetal grey, like Create's girders and industrial iron (Taran).

**Gearboxes.** Create's gearbox is the andesite gearbox, so it now has the andesite top speed (128 RPM). Wooden, copper and iron gearboxes relay and mirror rotation exactly like it, with their material's limits, friction and weathering, and turn shaft halves of their own material. Recipes use Create's plus shape, four parts around a casing: wooden shafts around a stripped log, copper cogwheels around copper casing, iron cogwheels around Iron Casing (an industrial iron block before 0.23.0). There is no separate vertical item: a gearbox stands upright, but lies on its side when a shaft above or below points into it (or when placed sneaking), turning to meet any shaft beside it the way Create's vertical gearbox does; a wrench turns it as usual. Looks: a riveted metal frame around a sunken plate (planks in a dark board frame for wood), patina and rust specks per stage, and a bushing on each open face. Why each matters: wood is the cheapest gearbox and a fuse; andesite is maintenance-free but slow; copper and iron are the only gearboxes for 256 RPM lines.

**Clutches and gearshifts.** Create's clutch and gearshift are built on andesite casing, so they join the andesite tier and now stop at 128 RPM too. Wooden, copper and iron clutches and gearshifts work exactly like Create's (powered, a clutch cuts off the far side and a gearshift reverses it), with their material's limits, friction and weathering, and turn shaft halves of their own material. Recipes follow Create's shapeless ones, the material's casing with a shaft (clutch) or a cogwheel (gearshift) and redstone: copper casing for copper, Iron Casing for iron (an industrial iron block before 0.23.0), a stripped log and a wooden shaft for a wooden clutch. Wood has no cogwheels, so a wooden gearshift is a wooden gearbox and redstone. Brass has neither since 0.23.0 (Taran); brass clutches and gearshifts from older worlds go quietly, and in inventories they become Create's. Looks: the gearbox's casing in the material, with Create's markings (a red band across a clutch, two red strips along a gearshift) that glow while powered, and an open frame on each end around the turning shaft. Why each matters: a wooden clutch is the cheapest switch and still a fuse; Create's are maintenance-free but slow; copper and iron are the only ones for 256 RPM lines.

**Chain drives.** Create's encased chain drive and adjustable chain gearshift are built on andesite casing as well, so they join the andesite tier and stop at 128 RPM. Before, a powered chain gearshift turned 128 RPM into 256 out of andesite parts alone; now every chain drive past a doubling gearshift has to take the faster speed. Copper and iron chain drives and chain gearshifts work exactly like Create's (a row of them relays rotation, and a powered chain gearshift turns the rest of its row up to twice as fast, by signal strength), with their material's limits, friction and weathering. One row can mix materials, and each drive in it keeps its own limit. Wood has no chain drives, and brass no chain gearshift since 0.23.0 (Taran). Recipes follow Create's shapeless ones: the material's casing and three of its nuggets for a chain drive (copper casing and copper nuggets, Iron Casing and iron nuggets), and a chain drive and an electron tube for a chain gearshift. Looks: the gearbox's casing in the material, laid out like Create's, with a chain of the material's own links running round the middle in a channel and, on the faces the shaft comes out of, from behind the bushing to the next drive in the row. A chain gearshift has a redstone window over its chain that glows while powered, and goggles on a powered one say how much faster the rest of its row turns. Why each matters: Create's are maintenance-free but carry only 128 RPM; copper and iron are the only chain drives for 256 RPM lines and the only ones that can sit past a powered gearshift on a 128 RPM line. Belts, speedometers and stressometers keep no speed limit, so a fast line can always be belted and measured.

### Machine tiers (0.17.0)

Part 2 of Taran's friction redesign (project memory `machine-tiers.md`). The machines that make things, Create's Mechanical Press, Mechanical Mixer, Millstone, Encased Fan, Mechanical Saw, Mechanical Drill, Deployer, Mechanical Crafter and Mechanical Arm, also come in copper, iron, steel and aluminium (and titanium since 0.19.0). Create's own count as andesite, its crafter and arm as brass. Since 0.23.0 only brass makes the Mechanical Crafter (Taran): the other crafters are gone, quietly from old worlds, and in inventories they become Create's.

- **The same machine in another casing.** Each tier is Create's machine with its casing's colours: Create's models and animations, its textures recoloured for every weathering stage, and the tier's own casing, gearbox face or chain drive. It works exactly as Create's does, Create's Ponder scenes and JEI tabs list it, and Create's code takes it for its own wherever it asks about its machines: chutes about fans, arms about what they reach, crafter grids and covers, placement helpers, contraptions and schematics.
- **Limits.** A machine is a support held to its own material's top speed: brass 96 RPM, andesite 128, copper and iron 256, steel and aluminium 512, titanium 2,048 (since 0.19.0). Past it, it grinds and breaks, dropping itself. Since 0.27.0 (Taran's card) it takes any load, as every support does, and only the shafts and cogs driving it give way; until then it carried only its material's (brass 3,072 su, copper 4,096, aluminium 8,192, the rest any). It draws its material's friction, iron and steel being heavier to turn.
- **Weathering.** Copper machines oxidize anywhere and iron and steel ones rust only where wet, each stage adding a little friction, as parts do. Honeycomb waxes one, an axe scrapes off wax and then patina. Aluminium never weathers.
- **Swapping.** Using a casing on a placed machine makes it that material, if the machine comes in it, a step down as well as up (Taran). It keeps its facing, what it holds and how it is set (a drill its bits), comes out fresh and unwaxed, and the casing it had pops out of the face clicked. A deployer's front and a crafter's front still take a casing in hand, as in Create, and a deployer holding a casing swaps the machine it faces.
- **Crafting.** Create's recipe with the tier's casing in place of the andesite or brass one: copper casing, Iron Casing (an Industrial Iron Block before 0.23.0), Steel Casing, Aluminium Casing or Titanium Casing. The Aluminium Casing needs polyethylene, so aluminium machines come after stage four.
- **Crushing wheels** stay Create's andesite only (Taran: no other material makes sense for them). They hold no shafts, so they are no support and carry any load, but still turn no faster than 128 RPM, and their tooltip shows that Stability (Steady).
- **Vat mixers.** Stage four's vat form of a mixer is its mixer's material: copper, iron, steel and aluminium mixers each stir vats of their own metal, and since 0.20.0 Create's own mixer has none and works only over basins (Taran). It tops out at its mixer's speed, draws its friction and weathers as it does. Since 0.23.0 it is a plate of the vat's own tank with a cog on its face, and turns by that cog from the cogs beside it, as Create's mixer does; no shaft goes into it (Taran).

### Heat and cooling (0.18.0)

Part 3 of Taran's friction redesign (project memory `friction-cooling.md`, cards in `friction-heat-cards.md`). Only aluminium supports heated up in 0.18.0; titanium's do too since 0.19.0 (Titanium, below).

- **Friction heat.** An aluminium support warms as it turns and cools off on its own. Its temperature, in °C, is kept by Power Grid's thermal model, so heat sinks, water coolers, fans and Power Grid's thermometer treat it as they treat a Power Grid device. Only what holds the part heats (Taran's card): an aluminium bracket, casing, gearbox, clutch, gearshift, chain drive or machine heats whatever it holds, while an aluminium shaft in a steel bracket stays cool. It makes 8.27 W per RPM and sheds 3.64 W per degree above the air, so left alone it runs whole up to about 256 RPM, settling near 600 °C there, and at 512 RPM it melts about a minute after starting cold (Taran's card). Goggles show its temperature, gold from 520 °C and red from 620.
- **Melting.** From 520 °C it glows orange and smokes, from 620 it drips, and at 660 it melts as aluminium beside lava does: the aluminium runs down and sets as rough aluminium, off to one side where the part it held still stands over it. A bracket melts off the part it held and a casing off the part inside, which stay only while another support holds them; a gearbox, clutch, gearshift or chain drive melts whole, and a machine melts whole and drops what it held.
- **Heat sinks and fans.** A heat sink bolted on, base against it, carries it to about 384 RPM, and a fan of 128 RPM or more blowing through the sink's fins carries it to aluminium's top speed of 512 (Taran's card). An encased fan blowing straight at it with no sink cools it about as much as one sink in still air and never more (Taran: "a little on its own"), so it carries it to about 384 too.
- **Water coolers.** A heat sink's base with a copper water jacket, bolted on like a heat sink to an aluminium support or a Power Grid device (Taran's card); pipes join it on any face but its base, into two tanks of a bucket each, one for what comes in and one for what goes out. It takes heat in step with how far above the air what it cools is, 15 W per degree on water, and warms the water as it does, so it uses water only as fast as it cools (Taran's card): 134 J turns a millibucket of water into warm water. The warm water has to be pumped away, or it stops when full, and with no water it cools nothing. Recipe: a heat sink, two fluid pipes and two copper sheets (Taran's card).
- **Cold water.** Twice as fast as water in a water cooler (30 W per degree) and 188 J a millibucket, coming out warm. Radiators make it, and it is drawn wherever it is cold enough to snow at that height (Taran: any snowy biome, its lakes and springs as well as its frozen rivers), and from any biome in `mcremix:cold_water`, which holds every biome tagged `forge:is_snowy` (since 0.19.0: WWOO makes it rain in patches of snowy plains, taigas and beaches and all over ice spikes, groves and frozen rivers, and they still give cold water), by bucket, pump or hose pulley; the sea still gives salt water, frozen oceans too. It stays cold wherever it is piped (Taran).
- **Radiators.** Thin aluminium fins on copper tubes between a front and a back plate. Radiators side by side in one plane, facing the same way, join into a panel that shares what it holds as a fluid tank does (Taran picked joined panels), up to 256 blocks; pipes join it on its front or back, on any of its blocks. It chills any water into cold water (Taran, in words: any water, since that just makes sense) and hot coolant into coolant, lets cold water and coolant straight through, and does not take salt water. Each block chills 0.5 mB a tick in still air, times the cooling of the fans blowing through it: a 128 RPM fan right under it more than doubles that, and Create's fans blow on through radiators, so a fan under each column of a panel, blowing up, cools the whole column. Recipe: six aluminium sheets round three fluid pipes make two (Taran's card).
- **Coolant** (Taran's ask: a coolant that makes a radiator loop worth building). Ethylene dichloride and caustic soda mixed over heat make ethylene glycol and salt water, and glycol mixed with water makes coolant, bright green. In a water cooler it takes heat three times as fast as water (45 W per degree, 188 J a millibucket) and comes out as hot coolant, a paler green; a radiator chills hot coolant back at the rate it chills warm water, and coolant never wears out (Taran's four cards). Water is free to run through once and pour away, while coolant has to be made, so it goes round a loop.
- **Poured out.** Warm and cold water poured out of a bucket, an open pipe end or a hose pulley become plain water, and hot coolant becomes coolant, as deep as they were.
- **Off switch.** The server config's `frictionHeat` (on by default) turns friction heat off.

### Titanium (0.19.0)

Idea bank stage 5 and Taran's ask of 2026-10-23: titanium as the highest tier, faster than anything, needing more cooling than aluminium. Cards in project memory `titanium-cards.md` and `titanium-chain-cards.md`; Claude's own calls in `titanium-build-choices.md`.

- **Parts.** Titanium comes in every part (shaft, cog, large cog, bracket, gearbox, clutch, gearshift, chain drive, chain gearshift), as a casing and in every machine. It turns up to 2,048 RPM (Taran: not unlimited, 2,048; Create's own cap is raised to match), carries any load, holds parts six blocks out (three until 0.27.0), has no drag and never weathers. Its supports take polyethylene: the titanium casing is a titanium sheet, a stripped log and a Polyethylene Bushing, and the gearbox, clutch, gearshift and chain drives are built on it.
- **Heat.** Its supports heat as aluminium's do, only what holds the part, but need twice the cooling (Taran): 25.33 W of friction per RPM, so with nothing on it titanium holds about 128 RPM. It glows from 800 °C and goggles show how far it is from burning.
- **Cooling.** A heat sink is little use: its aluminium melts past 660 °C into rough aluminium (Taran's card), so a sink holds titanium only to about 150 RPM and a fan through it to about 225. Water coolers have no such limit: about 700 RPM on water, 1,300 on cold water, 1,900 on coolant, and 2,048 on two coolers of coolant.
- **Burning.** Past 1,000 °C (Taran, 2026-10-21: titanium burns when it overheats) it flares white for three seconds, setting fire to anything that burns across an air gap beside it, then crumbles into titanium dioxide powder, one for each ingot's worth of metal in it. A bracket burns off its part and a casing off the part inside, which stay only while another support holds them; a gearbox, clutch, gearshift, chain drive or machine burns whole. The powder does for rutile again, and makes white dye.
- **Temper colours** (Taran: the tint stays, like the real metal). Past 400 °C what holds a titanium part turns gold, past 550 purple and past 700 blue, and keeps the deepest it reached when it cools. Only hydrochloric acid, poured beside it, strips it back to plain. The colour is only for looks; a part swaps to its coloured block without stopping. Since 0.22.0 the colours are faint, the grey showing through them (Taran: more subtle).
- **The chain** (Taran's cards: the full chain, every pick Claude's). Basalt crushed or milled is **black sand**, and Power Grid's electromagnet pulls its iron out as nuggets, leaving **rutile**. Salt water in a heated basin with no mixer or press over it **boils** down to **salt** (500 mB a lump); the crucible melts salt into **molten salt** (Hot), and an Electrolyser splits that into **molten sodium** and **chlorine**. A press binds rutile and coal coke with coal tar into **Rutile Briquettes**; chlorine run over one in a Steel Furnace at Blazing comes off as **titanium tetrachloride**, a gas that fumes into white smoke and hydrogen chloride mist if let loose. Stirred in a steel vat, titanium tetrachloride and molten sodium leave **titanium sponge** and salt, which goes round again. Molten sodium meeting water in the world goes off with a bang and a burst of fire that breaks no blocks (Taran's card).
- **The Arc Furnace** (Taran's card: an arc over a water-cooled copper hearth). A Power Grid device: a copper hearth under a Bakelite beam on steel posts, a Carbon Electrode hanging from under each of its two terminals. Its arc is a quarter-ohm load with no polarity (20 V drive 80 A, 1.6 kW); it melts only from 400 W, 16 kJ for each sponge, into **titanium ingots**. Its hearth sheds only 150 W by itself and overheats at Power Grid's 175 °C, so it needs a water cooler (one on water holds about 2.5 kW). Both electrodes burn away, 160 kJ each. Hands, funnels and arms load it; hoppers empty it.
- **The titanium Pressurizer** (0.19.1; Taran, stage 4: steel now, titanium later). The Pressurizer in titanium: up to 2,048 RPM where the steel one stops at 512, so its jobs run up to four times as fast, at the same 32 su per RPM. It heats, tints and burns like any titanium machine, so past about 128 RPM it wants a water cooler. Its vessel shows the temper colour its Pressurizer has from heat; until 0.28.0 the vessel also weathered on its own, as if it were copper, and turned gold, purple and blue with no heat (Taran).
- **Titanium plating** (Taran's card: decorative plate tinted in a furnace). Four titanium sheets make four plating. A Furnace fires it to the deepest colour its heat reaches, gold at Hot, purple at Searing and blue at Blazing, and a colour only goes deeper; acid strips it back to plain.

### Weathering

Copper and iron weather fast, and not all the way, so a build takes on a varied palette within a day of play instead of turning one flat green over weeks. Every block with copper or rust stages counts, whichever mod adds it: vanilla's copper blocks, cut copper, stairs and slabs; Create's copper shingles and tiles, with their stairs and slabs; this mod's copper, iron and steel parts; since 0.23.0 the copper on water coolers, radiators and arc furnaces, for looks only (Taran); and, with Immersive Weathering, its cut and plate iron, with their stairs and slabs, and the iron doors, trapdoors and bars it makes rust.

- **Fast.** On each random tick a block moves on a stage with a chance that takes it from fresh to fully weathered in half a day on average (`weatheringDays`, 0.5; a day is 20 minutes), so about nine blocks in ten reach their final stage within a day.
- **By itself.** Vanilla holds a copper block back while any copper within four blocks of it is fresher, which keeps a big build waiting for its slowest block. Here each block weathers on its own.
- **Not all the way.** Each spot has a final stage, weathered or fully oxidized (rusted for iron), half of spots each, picked at random from the world seed and the position, so it never changes: a block weathers to it and stops. Until 0.23.0 a third of spots stopped at exposed; Taran asked for copper and iron always to get at least to the second-to-last stage. An axe scrapes a block back a stage and it weathers to the same stage again; honeycomb keeps it as it is. (`variedWeathering` off: everything goes all the way.)
- **Iron.** Iron rusts only while wet: rained on, waterlogged or beside water. Indoor machines stay free of friction, and Immersive Weathering's iron under a roof stays clean, so a build rusts where the weather reaches it (`ironRustsWhenDry` on: Immersive Weathering's iron rusts anywhere, as copper oxidizes; this mod's parts still need water).
- **Rust runs down.** Dry iron under rustier iron rusts as if wet, but never past the block above it, so rust rained on at the top of a column or wall works its way down as far as the blocks above it got, fading wherever a block stopped early. Any rusting iron counts, this mod's parts and Immersive Weathering's alike (a door goes by what is on its upper half); waxed iron is sealed and lets none run off, and copper above iron does nothing.
- **Steel (0.28.0).** Steel rusts where this mod's iron does, and only while wet, but never past its first stage (Taran): its parts and machines as before, and now its pipes, pumps, electric pumps and tanks, and so the vats and boilers they make, gauges and all. Steel that rusted further before 0.28.0, waxed or not, goes back to its first stage on its next random tick, and those later stages are no longer in the creative tab or JEI. Rusted steel never leaks; only iron rusted through does.
- **Switching it off.** With `fastWeathering` off every block keeps its own mod's rules again: vanilla's pace and neighbour rule for copper, Immersive Weathering's for its iron, and the old pace for this mod's iron parts, wet or under rustier iron.

Every random tick goes through one mixin (`BlockStateBaseMixin`) into `kinetics/Weathering`, so the rules reach blocks from any mod that uses vanilla's copper stages or Immersive Weathering's rust levels. A door weathers by its lower half, and Immersive Weathering's doors bring the upper half along. Create's industrial iron and weathered iron are separate blocks with no stages between them, so they stay as they are.

### Drill bits and saw blades (pickaxe and axe tiers for every drill and saw)

Since 0.17.0, bits and blades are items fitted to any drill or saw, Create's and every tier's, in place of the stone, golden and diamond drills and saws of earlier versions (gone, with no conversion). A drill's or saw's body sets its top speed; its bits or blade decide what it cuts, how fast and for what stress. Bits cut whatever a pickaxe of their material could mine, and a blade fells what an axe of its material would harvest, at that tool's speed relative to iron (vanilla's mining speeds: stone 4, iron 6, diamond 8, gold 12). Every drill and saw comes with iron ones, so Create's drill no longer cuts obsidian, crying obsidian or ancient debris (a server config switch gives that back).

| Bits or blade | Cuts like | Speed | Stress | Stonecutting (blade) | Recipe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | stone pickaxe or axe | ×2/3 | 2 su per RPM | yes | three cobblestone over an iron ingot (bits) or an iron sheet (blade) |
| Iron (what every drill and saw comes with) | iron | ×1 | 4 su per RPM | yes | three iron ingots over the same |
| Gold | golden (only blocks that need no tier) | ×2 | 2 su per RPM | no: gold is too soft for stone | three gold ingots over the same |
| Diamond | diamond | ×4/3 | 8 su per RPM | yes | three diamonds over the same |

- **Fitting.** Use bits or a blade on a placed drill or saw to fit them; the old ones pop out of the face clicked. A deployer holding them fits them too. They never wear out (Taran).
- **Breaking.** A broken drill or saw keeps what it had fitted (Taran): the item names its bits or blade and goes back down with them, and only iron-fitted ones stack with new ones. A casing swap keeps them too.
- **Contraptions** read the bits from the block's data, so a piston pushing a diamond-bit drill cuts obsidian, and the stress follows the bits.

Why each matters: stone bits and blades are the first (bloom iron comes later) and stay the cheapest and lightest way to cut stone, coal, iron and copper and to cut stone on a belt; gold cuts stone, deepslate, netherrack and coal and strips and planks logs twice as fast for half the stress, the bits for cobblestone generators and quarries and the blade for a tree farm, but stops at iron, copper and lapis ore and cuts no stone; iron cuts every ore; only diamond bits cut obsidian, crying obsidian and ancient debris, and the diamond blade is the fastest stonecutter, at twice the stress. There is no netherite, which would only be a better diamond. The tier test is Forge's tier sorting (vanilla's `needs_*_tool` tags), so modded blocks follow their tags.

Behaviour and looks: a fixed drill facing a block its bits cannot cut never starts on it. About twice a second its bits skate over the face, throwing sparks, crits and grit with a high grinding squeal, so a bank of drills shows which are stuck; looking at a stuck drill (no goggles needed) says "Too hard for gold bits" and which bits would cut the block. On a contraption the drill leaves such a block standing, so a piston or gantry stops there as at bedrock, with a burst of sparks. Stone on a golden blade skates across it for half a second with the same squeal, then carries on uncut, so a mixed belt through a gold-bladed saw planks the logs and passes the stone along; a blade grinds against modded wood that needs a harder axe and leaves it standing. The speed applies to everything a saw does: items on an upward saw, trees it fells in front of it, and felling on a contraption. Goggles name the bits or blade and their speed. Fitted bits give the drill head their texture (cobblestone, gold block, diamond block; iron keeps Create's own head), and blades are redrawn and animated: a chipped grey stone disc, a bright gold blade with hooked teeth, and a steel disc set with diamond segments.

### Fluids, heat and the crucible (0.13.0)

**Fluids have a heat**, on the furnace's scale: water and most fluids are Cold, lava is Hot, and the molten metals melt at their real heats: zinc and aluminium Hot; copper, gold and brass Searing; iron, steel and netherite Blazing (fluid tags `mcremix:heat/*`; an untagged fluid goes by its temperature). **Everything that holds or carries fluid has a ceiling** (block tags `mcremix:fluid_ceiling/*`; untagged blocks hold anything):

| Material | Pipes | Pumps | Tanks | Holds up to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brass (0.20.0) | yes: thin, 6 px across where Create's are 8, and a line with one in it carries half as much | 96 RPM | yes, and ordinary boilers | Hot |
| Glass | yes: a quarter of copper's flow, never corrodes, shows the fluid running through it | no | no | Hot |
| Copper (Create's own pipes, pumps and tanks, and its smart pipes, valves, spouts and drains) | yes | 256 RPM | yes | Hot |
| Iron (0.20.0) | yes: rusts, and rusted through leaks | 256 RPM | yes, and ordinary boilers | Hot |
| Steel | yes: since 0.28.0 rusts to its first stage when wet, and never leaks | 512 RPM | yes, and compact pressure boilers | Searing |
| Aluminium (0.20.0) | yes: never weathers, melts beside anything Hot | 512 RPM on a polyethylene bushing, heating with friction | yes, but never a boiler | Warm |
| PVC (0.16.0) | thin face pipes only, lying on block faces; a pump reaches twice as far through them | no | no | Cold |

Nothing carries a Blazing fluid: the crucible pours iron, steel and netherite instead. Past its ceiling a part glows and smokes for about three seconds, then bursts and drops as an item, as an overspeeding shaft does: glass shatters, PVC sags and bursts, aluminium melts (about five seconds, as aluminium parts do) into rough aluminium, and a tank gives way in every block it has. What it carried spills out of the gap. Tanks of different materials never join. Create's copper pipes, glass pipes, pumps and tanks now weather like copper blocks, for looks only (no friction, no loss of flow): wax them with honeycomb, scrape them with an axe, and they keep their patina when broken. Steel and aluminium never weather. Item tooltips say how hot a fluid each part holds. The new parts are Create's own models in redrawn textures.

**Pipe and tank metals (0.20.0).** Taran took the wooden pipes, pumps and tanks out (and Create's mixer's vat form with them) and added brass, iron and aluminium ones; the cards are in project memory `pipe-tank-rework-cards.md`.
- **Brass** pipes are thin and fine, like the brass clockwork parts: Create's pipe drawn 6 px across, rims and all, with a thinner hitbox. A line with a brass pipe anywhere in it moves half as much each tick (the slowest pipe sets the pace, glass a quarter). The brass pump turns up to 96 RPM; since 0.27.0 it takes any load, as every support does (until then only brass's 3,072 su). Since 0.26.0 (Taran) the **Brass Mechanical Pump** has a handwheel on a stem over its body: held right-click on it opens a slider setting the most a line through the pump moves, from 1 to 24 mB a tick (24 is all a brass pump moves at 96 RPM through brass, as Create's pump moves half its speed in mB a tick and a brass line goes at half pace). Placed before 0.26.0, it lets all through until it is turned down. Brass tanks make Create's ordinary boilers, and vats like any tank, but no mixer fits a brass vat.
- **Iron** holds what copper holds and rusts as this mod's iron does: only in the weather (rain, water beside it, or rustier iron above), never from what runs inside, each block to a final stage of its own, with Immersive Weathering's rust names (exposed, weathered, rusted). **Rusted through, it drips, and can leak** (Taran): since 0.22.1 it only drips unless the server config's `rustedIronLeaks` is on (Taran: off by default). With it on, every unwaxed iron pipe, pump or tank block at its last stage loses 1 mB a tick, a pipe or pump while fluid goes through it (taken from where the line draws) and a tank block while its tank holds anything (from a vat, its heaviest liquid first). Liquids drip from it and are lost; gases get out as their clouds do from anything open, a second's worth at a time. Scraping it with an axe, cleaning it with acid or waxing it stops the leak and the dripping, and a rusted part keeps its rust when broken. Iron tanks make Create's ordinary boilers, and an iron mixer fits an iron vat.
- **Aluminium** never weathers, but holds only Warm fluids: with anything Hot inside, or beside anything Hot (lava, fire, a lit Furnace, a Blaze Burner, molten metal), a pipe, pump or tank glows and drips for about five seconds and melts into rough aluminium, a tank's metal setting after what it held has spilled. The aluminium pump turns up to 512 RPM on a polyethylene bushing, like every aluminium support, and heats with friction as they do. Aluminium tanks make no boiler; an aluminium mixer fits an aluminium vat.
- **Recipes** (fixed in 0.20.0: from 0.13.0 nothing made this mod's pipes, pumps or tanks, so the Pressurizer, which takes a steel tank, could not be made in survival) follow Create's copper ones in each metal: a sheet either side of an ingot makes four pipes (across or upright), a pipe and the metal's cogwheel make a pump (the aluminium one with a polyethylene bushing too), and a sheet above and below a barrel makes a tank. Iron's upright grids are already Create's chute and item vault, so iron pipes are made across only and an iron tank has its sheets either side of the barrel. Glass pipes are a pane either side of a block of glass. A Spout of hydrochloric acid cleans a rusted iron pipe, pump or tank item back to bare iron.
- **Each metal keeps to itself** (Taran, 0.23.0, card B). A plain pipe joins only pipes of its own metal: copper (Create's pipe, encased or windowed), brass, iron, glass, steel, aluminium or PVC. Two pipes of different metals side by side do not connect, and each has a rim there. Fittings join any metal (Create's pumps, valves and smart pipes, and this mod's pumps), so one between two metals is the adapter, and tanks and machines take any pipe. Pipes of two metals that joined in an older world come apart as it loads.
- **Steam engines in each boiler metal** (Taran, 0.24.0, cards C and D). Brass, iron and steel have steam engines of their own, made as Create's is with their metal's block in place of the copper block. Each goes only on a tank of its own metal, and Create's only on Create's copper tanks: placing one against another metal's tank says which boilers it goes on. Whatever its metal, an engine runs as Create's does, as fast and as strong from each boiler level; the metal changes the boiler, not the engine (a steel boiler is compact and bursts without a whistle). There is no aluminium engine, since aluminium tanks make no boiler. Create's engines already on brass, iron or steel boilers in an older world become that metal's engine. Since 0.26.0 (Taran) an engine drives a shaft of any material, as Create's drives its own: it holds nothing, so the shaft needs a bracket or support within reach like any bare part. Once an engine is on a boiler, the boiler's gauge is drawn in its tanks' metal, rusting with iron tanks and weathering with Create's copper ones.
- **Electric pumps** (Taran, 0.24.0). Power Grid's electric pump is the copper one; iron, steel and aluminium have their own, each a Power Grid electric motor on a pipe of its metal (the brass one went in 0.26.0, Taran, for the Brass Mechanical Pump's handwheel; one placed before is Power Grid's pump now). They draw what Power Grid's draws and go faster the more power they take, up to their metal's pump speed: 256 RPM in iron (as copper), 512 in steel and aluminium. Each is rated for a fifth more power than its top speed takes, as Power Grid's is, and overheats on much more, so a steel pump runs on volts that would burn Power Grid's. They hold what their metal holds and weather or melt as its pipes do: iron rusts and drips, aluminium melts beside anything Hot. Like every pump they join pipes of any metal.
- **Old worlds.** Wooden pipes, pumps and tanks, the Steel Spring and Create's mixer's vat form vanish from worlds and inventories without Forge's missing-block warning; slag roads turn back into dirt paths (`RemovedBlocks`).

**Steel pressure boilers.** A boiler of steel tanks is compact: a boiler level for every two tanks where Create's needs four. Since 0.26.0 (Taran) it boils no water and takes no heat from what is under it: pressurised steam piped in from a Pressurizer is all that runs it (see Plastics below), and its goggles show its size and that steam in place of water and heat. Steam its engines do not use builds pressure. A steam whistle on it vents the spare steam, screaming while it does; without one the boiler hisses and groans, then bursts (one spare level bursts it in 90 seconds, nine in ten): its tanks fall as items, what they held is lost, and the steam scalds anyone within four blocks. Nothing around it breaks.

**Molten metal.** Eight molten metals (iron, gold, copper, zinc, brass, steel, netherite, and aluminium since 0.14.0), measured as most mods measure them: 90 mB an ingot, 10 a nugget, 810 a block. Each has a bucket. Spilled, it runs like slow lava, sets fire to what it touches, and after a while sets into **Rough Metal**, layered like snow to the height it stopped at, which holds the metal and mines back into ingots and nuggets (netherite has no nuggets, so what is short of an ingot is lost). Water sets it at once with a hiss. A **Block of Steel** and a **Block of Aluminium** (nine ingots) exist for block molds.

**The Crucible.** Seven clay balls shaped like a pot make an Unfired Crucible, fired Hot in the Furnace. It hangs on a shaft between two posts, its lip facing whoever placed it, and melts what is put in it (by hand, funnel, chute, hopper or arm) with the heat of the block underneath, as a boiler is heated: zinc and aluminium at Hot; copper, gold and brass at Searing; iron, steel and netherite only over a superheated Blaze Burner. Every level hotter than a metal needs halves its time. It melts metal, nuggets, sheets, blocks, raw ore and crushed ore, an ingot's worth (90 mB) a piece (recipe type `mcremix:melting`). **Tools and armour** melt too, into half the metal they were made of, worn or not, and enchanted or not (Taran, 0.14.0): iron and gold tools and armour, shears, chainmail (as much iron as iron armour), iron and gold horse armour (a chestplate's worth), and Create's copper diving helmet, boots and backtank. Netherite gear, Create's included, gives back half its netherite ingot (45 mB); the diamonds or copper under it are lost. It holds 810 mB, and accepts a piece only while its metal still fits. **Alloys** form from what is in it (`mcremix:alloying`): copper and zinc as brass (Searing, as Create mixes it), four ingots of iron and a Coal Coke as four of steel (Blazing, a quarter of a cementation pack's time; blister steel also melts as steel), and four gold and four netherite scrap as netherite (Blazing).
- **Pouring.** Turning its shaft tips it: a full turn of the shaft lays it on its side, and turning back stands it up. It pours whatever rises over the lip (the more it is tipped, the less it keeps), oldest metal first, at 15 mB a tick, onto whatever is in front of the lip: a Sand Mold on a depot or belt, another crucible, anything with a tank, or the ground, where it spills. A tipped crucible kept full pours as fast as it melts. Comparators read how full it is; goggles show its heat, contents, what is melting and how far it is tipped.
- **Sand Molds.** A Mechanical Press in a basin rams sand round an ingot (or a brick) into a Sand Ingot Mold, or four sand round a block into a Sand Block Mold, and gives the pattern back. Under the stream a mold fills with one metal (90 or 810 mB); full, it sets for two seconds, then the casting breaks out and its sand falls away. A mold the stream left short keeps its metal to be topped up later, or breaks open in the hand for nuggets. A full mold carried away sets in the inventory, or on the ground. Since 0.28.0 (Taran) JEI shows every metal cast into both molds, on a Casting tab.
- **Big crucibles.** 27 crucibles placed as a 3x3x3 cube join into one big crucible (Taran: only 1x1x1 or 3x3x3, so pouring makes sense), hung on the shafts at the middles of two opposite sides and pouring from the middle of its front. It holds 810 mB per block, needs heat under every one of its nine columns, melts nine times as fast and pours nine times as fast; turning it is a heavy load. Broken, it falls apart into crucibles that share out its metal.
- A crucible never wears out, and molten metal stays molten in it even when the heat goes (a simplification).

### Power Grid, chemistry and aluminium (0.14.0)

Taran's rules for this stage: Create: Power Grid becomes a required dependency, with no furnace stand-ins, and one stage of progression needs a real, working circuit built with it; Bakelite is a crafting ingredient only, never a parts tier. Taran answered the stage's questions on decision cards (the memory file `idea-bank-stage3.md` has every answer) and then redesigned electrolysis: no cell block, but an Electrolyser on a basin or a vat.

**Power Grid's own recipes.** Its electric motor and transformer core take steel, and its blank circuit boards (sawn from Bakelite), switches, buttons and sockets take Bakelite. Its etched boards are etched in ferric chloride instead of its acid.

**Coal tar and wood spirit.** Coking coal gives off 50 mB of coal tar per coal, and charring logs 50 mB of wood spirit per log, into a tank in the Furnace (firing recipes name a `byproduct`). Pipes draw it off from any side, or a bucket fills from it. The tank holds a bucket of one of them; past that, or while it holds the other one, the rest burns away at the chimney in black smoke, and the firing carries on either way. A Furnace takes no fuel by pipe: since 0.16.0 piped fuel is the Steel Furnace's. **Bakelite**, the first plastic, is 100 mB of each pressed together in a heated basin (Create compacting).

**Salt water** comes only from the sea (Taran): water taken in an ocean or beach biome, by bucket, pump or hose pulley, is salt water.

**Chlorine** is a gas, and runs in any pipe or tank (Taran: no corrosion). Let loose, from a basin, an open pipe end or a burst tank, it becomes a low yellow-green cloud, heavier than air, that sinks to the ground and lingers: a bucket's worth hangs about for half a minute, three blocks across, and more gas makes it wider and longer-lived. Once a second it hurts anything inside that breathes (a heart; not the undead or golems), and it kills the plants under it: grass, mycelium, podzol and moss go to dirt, and flowers, crops, saplings, leaves, vines, cacti and cane die away. Its use: an iron nugget, 250 mB of chlorine and 250 of water mix into **ferric chloride**, which etches Power Grid's circuit boards. Since 0.16.0 a basin holds no gas, so that mixing is done in a vat, by a mixer on its side.

**Hydrogen** is a gas too, and fuel (Taran). In 0.14.0 it burned in the Furnace's firebox at Searing; since 0.16.0 only the Steel Furnace burns piped fuel, and hydrogen is one of its two Blazing fuels: 4 mB a tick keeps it Searing and 8 Blazing. Escaped hydrogen vents harmlessly with a hiss (Taran turned down a flame). Why it is fuel and not free energy: an anode is good for 12 kJ (below). Driven gently, at about 3.5 V, that splits about 34 batches of salt water, four buckets of hydrogen; at 27 A about three, and at the Electrolyser's rating about two. An anode costs two Coal Coke and 100 mB of tar; four buckets of hydrogen keep a Steel Furnace Searing for under a minute, where two Coal Coke keep a Furnace Searing for over five, so hydrogen gives back less than its anodes cost, while the current that split it comes from rotation that could have done other work. What it buys is Blazing heat. A salt-water works can fire its own furnaces, but it cannot power a base.

**Aluminium, from red sand.** Each step can be worked out from what you see:
1. Washing red sand with a fan turns up **bauxite** a quarter of the time, beside Create's gold nuggets.
2. Two bauxite dissolve in 250 mB of **caustic soda** in a heated basin (Create mixing) into 250 mB of **bauxite liquor**, leaving **red mud**. Caustic soda comes from splitting salt water, so the first of it comes from the sea.
3. Left to stand cool, the liquor **settles** out **aluminium hydroxide** and gives back 100 mB of caustic soda (10 seconds a batch, recipe type `mcremix:settling`). A basin settles one batch at a time, only with no heat under it and no mixer or press over it; a vat settles a batch for every bucket it holds, up to 16 at once.
4. The hydroxide fires at Searing into **alumina**.
5. An Electrolyser splits alumina into 90 mB of **molten aluminium**, with no heat under it (Taran: the current keeps the melt hot). It is piped off into a crucible and cast in sand molds; ingots press into sheets, and a sheet on a stripped log makes **Aluminium Casing**, for aluminium gearboxes, clutches, gearshifts and chain drives.

Red mud fires Warm into **red mud bricks**, a building block of its own, with stairs, slabs and walls (Taran); since 0.22.0 they are vanilla's mud bricks tinted red (Taran).

**The Electrolyser** (Taran's design) is a Power Grid device: two copper ingots and a Bakelite over three steel sheets. Its electrodes go into the block it is placed against: the side of a basin (since 0.23.0, Taran; on top of it until then) or of a vat. A **Carbon Electrode** in hand fits one, the anode (+) first, and an empty hand takes one out; funnels and arms fit them too. Since 0.23.0 the electrodes stand out of clamps on its two terminals, so what is fitted shows (Taran). Since 0.26.0 (Taran: fewer red, blue and brown pixels) it is a moulded black Bakelite head on its steel plate, its terminals Bakelite collars each marked by one thin red or blue band, with tinned clamps. One left on top of a basin from an older world is fixed to nothing (goggles say so) until it is taken off and put on a side.
- **Volts to start, amps to work** (Taran). Wired across a source, + to + and - to -, it is a real cell: next to nothing flows until about 4 V are across it, and past that the current, and how fast it splits, climb steeply (Power Grid's p-n junction, with a 0.02 Ω melt). A generator straight across one drives a huge current and cooks it, so it wants a transformer stepping the voltage down, or several Electrolysers in series on one source (two in series at 10 V settle at about 27 A, 4.9 V each). Wired the wrong way round, nothing flows.
- **Charge.** Each batch takes a set charge, amps times seconds: 100 C splits 250 mB of salt water into 125 mB each of chlorine, hydrogen and caustic soda, and 500 C splits an alumina into an ingot's worth of molten aluminium (recipe type `mcremix:electrolysis`).
- **Wear.** The anode burns away by the power through it, whatever it splits (Taran): 12 kJ burns one out. Pushing more current raises the voltage too, so a hard-driven Electrolyser costs more anode for each batch than a gently driven one: at 27 A an anode splits about five alumina. The cathode wears a tenth as fast (Taran). A burnt-out electrode is gone, and the Electrolyser stops until a new one goes in.
- **Heat.** It takes 600 W for good, about 100 A; past that it heats up until it overheats, as any Power Grid device does. Heat sinks bolted on let it take more.
- Goggles show both electrodes' wear, the volts and amps, its temperature, and what is missing (electrodes, a basin or vat, something to split, current).

A **Carbon Electrode** is two Coal Coke bound with 100 mB of coal tar by a press in a basin, then fired Hot in the Furnace.

**On a basin's side** it splits what is in the basin. The basin is open, so what it gives off as gas escapes over it (chlorine as a cloud, hydrogen with a hiss) and only the rest stays.

**A vat** (Taran's design) is like a steam boiler made of tanks: Create's, wooden or steel tanks, one to three wide and one to five high, standing on a whole layer of Create's andesite scaffolding.
- Pipes fill it through any of its tanks and draw off what it makes there, gases first, then liquids; what it was filled with only comes back out if it has nothing to do with it. Funnels, chutes and arms put items in through any tank.
- What it makes goes into three tanks of its own, each holding 2,000 mB for every tank block. Items it makes come out of the outlet under its cone into any inventory beside or under its scaffolding; since 0.28.0 a pipe runs from the outlet to each of them, and a funnel on the scaffolding, facing out, pulls them out too (Taran). When there is no room left, it stops until its pipes, or an inventory, take some away.
- Left alone it settles what will settle (and, since 0.16.0, ferments what will ferment). With Electrolysers on its sides it splits as fast as their current allows, and it is closed, so it keeps its gases. Since 0.16.0 it holds up to four fluids at once in layers, and mixers on its sides stir it (next section).

**The Heat Sink** is finned aluminium: four aluminium sheets over three ingots. Since 0.22.0 it is small (Taran): a base plate twelve pixels square, bolted down, with six fins standing half a block high. Placed on a Power Grid device, its base bolted to it, it draws the device's heat out (20 W for each degree between them) and sheds it to the air (2 W per degree above the air when still, far more with a fan blowing through its fins), so the device runs cooler and takes more power before it overheats. Several on one device add up. A sink beside a device, or with its base turned away, does nothing. Since 0.18.0 it cools aluminium supports the same way (Heat and cooling, above); on titanium it melts past 660 °C, so it helps titanium only a little (0.19.0).

**Aluminium parts melt beside anything Hot** (Taran), as in the Materials table: lava, fire, a lit Furnace, a Blaze Burner or molten metal. Which aluminium parts arrive in this stage was a question Taran moved to another thread; 0.14.0 has every kind of part, and a later decision there overrides that.

### Plastics, the Steel Furnace and PVC pipes (0.16.0)

Stage 4 of the idea bank. Taran answered seventeen questions first, then redesigned the chemistry in words (a Steel Furnace, a shaft-driven Pressurizer, vats holding several fluids, no gas in basins) and answered the cards on that, on mixers fitted to vats, on fluid fuel and on lighting furnaces: the memory files `idea-bank-stage4.md`, `stage4-redesign.md`, `vat-mixers-and-steel-furnace-fuel.md` and `furnace-lighting.md` have every answer, and `stage4-build-choices.md` lists the few numbers picked without a card (the spark plug's volts, the Pressurizer's recipes and stress). Polyethylene and both PVC products are crafting ingredients, never a parts tier; PVC pipes are the one exception Taran made.

**Lighting.** Both furnaces have to be lit, and a lit one burns its fuel whether or not it has work (Heat, above).

**The Steel Furnace** (Taran's design) is a Furnace cased in steel: Steel Casing used on a Furnace, or crafted with one. It keeps what its chamber held, but its solid fuel pops out and its fire goes out. It fires what a Furnace fires and heats a boiler or basin on top the same way, and it works fluids too (below). Its fire is **fuel pumped up into it from underneath**, as a burner is fed, and nothing else (Taran: the stone Furnace should stay useful early and be phased out later): no solid fuel, and it is sealed, so no heat comes up through it and Bellows do nothing for it.
- **Flow is heat.** Lit, it burns all the fuel that comes, and how hot depends on how many mB come a tick (reckoned over the last second) and on the fuel. Each level takes twice the heat of the one below, and each fuel stops at its own ceiling. Create's pumps move 1 mB a tick for every 2 RPM, so the speed of the pump feeding it sets its heat:

  | Fuel | Warm | Hot | Searing | Blazing |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Wood spirit | 1 mB/t | 2 (its ceiling) | | |
  | Ethanol | 1 | 2 | 4 (its ceiling) | |
  | Coal tar | | 1 | 2 (its ceiling) | |
  | Hydrogen | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |
  | Ethylene | | 1 | 2 | 4 |

  A pump never moves less than 1 mB a tick, so coal tar and ethylene, the richest, start at Hot. Coal tar is the thrifty fuel, and hydrogen and ethylene are the only ones that reach Blazing, where a Steel Furnace superheats a boiler or basin on top as a blaze burner fed Blaze Cake does. Lava is refused.
- **Too much, or unlit.** Fuel that comes faster than burns at the ceiling is lost up the chimney in black smoke (Taran: lost, not spilled), and goggles say so. A second after its fuel stops coming it goes out and has to be lit again. Fuel pumped into it unlit escapes out of its front (Taran): gases vent into the air, and liquids pool inside until a bucket's worth pours out as a fluid block to be cleaned up, backing the pipe up when there is nowhere for it to go. Lighting it then sends what had pooled up in a flare.
- **What it works.** A fluid one of its recipes takes, piped or poured in at its front, sides or top, waits in a 4,000 mB feed tank and is worked a batch at a time (recipe type `mcremix:steel_furnace`, each with a heat, every level hotter halving the time), into tanks of 4,000 mB that pipes draw off the same faces; finished items come out of those faces too, since the bottom is the fuel's. Ethanol run over **alumina** lying in the chamber at Hot or hotter turns to **ethylene**, 250 mB in 5 seconds, and an alumina lasts 16 buckets (Taran). At Searing it cracks **ethylene dichloride** into **vinyl chloride**, giving off as much **hydrogen chloride**.

**The Spark Plug** (Taran's idea) is a Power Grid device: a copper core in a Bakelite insulator in an iron shell, crafted as a copper ingot over Bakelite over an iron ingot, so it comes with Bakelite. It screws into a Furnace's or Steel Furnace's side or back, never its front, and pops off if the furnace goes or is turned to face it. It is a gap between + on its cap and - on its shell: nothing crosses until about 1,000 V are across it (it sparks from 900 V and holds down to 800, so a 1,000 V supply does it after what the wires lose), so it wants a transformer stepping the voltage up. Then it sparks through 100 kΩ, about 10 mA and 10 W, the whole time the voltage is there, lit furnace or not, and lights the furnace whenever there is fuel in it to catch (Taran: a switch or relay in its circuit decides when). It never wears out. Goggles show its volts, whether it is sparking and the furnace's state.

**Ethanol.** Two sugar in 250 mB of water ferment by themselves into 250 mB of ethanol in 30 seconds (Taran: no yeast), left to stand like settling (recipe type `mcremix:fermenting`): in a basin kept cool with nothing working it, a batch at a time, or in a vat, a batch for every bucket it holds at once. Ethanol is Steel Furnace fuel and the start of ethylene.

**Basins and gas.** A basin is open, so it holds no gas (Taran): gas piped into one, or made in it, goes off into the air above it once a second. Recipes that take a gas run in a vat.

**Vats of several fluids.** A vat holds up to four fluids at once, each a layer of its own sharing its space, the heaviest at the bottom and gases at the top (Taran), and goggles list the layers (until 0.21.0 its windows drew them). Pipes still draw off only what it makes, and what it was filled with only if it has nothing to do with it.

**The fermenter look (0.21.0).** Taran asked for vats to look like a brewery's conical fermenters, from a photo, and answered five cards (project memory `vat-fermenter-look.md`). The whole standing vat takes the look, in its own tanks' metal, weathering with them (copper's patina, iron's rust); loose tanks and scaffolding are unchanged. The tanks stay Create's square ones, with no dome on top (Taran). The layer of andesite scaffolding under it is drawn as a cone of the tank's plate metal, from a pixel in from its edge down to its middle, on four legs at the corners, braced together a little below the cone and standing on feet. Each side of its lowest tank layer has a manway door in the middle: a dark ring with a bolted plate on it, a cross handle on a hub and two hinges, standing out from the tank (Taran first chose one door a wrench moved round, then a door on every side). A door is left off where something is fitted in front of it, like a vat mixer, a pipe or an Electrolyser, so nothing pokes through. A standing vat is closed metal with no window (Taran): its tanks' windows shut as it forms and a wrench does nothing to them, and taken apart it opens them again if they were open before, unless the tank is a boiler by then. What it holds shows through goggles, which list every layer. The cone's outlet is only there when the vat has somewhere to empty into (Taran, 0.22.1): a pipe with a flange runs from the bottom of the cone down into an inventory under the middle of its scaffolding, and out sideways to one beside the middle of a side, one pipe for each (since 0.28.1 with no valve lever beside them, Taran); with none, the cone is closed at the bottom. The vat still empties into any inventory round its scaffolding, piped or not. It is all drawn into the blocks' models as their chunk is built (no block entity renderer), so it costs nothing per frame; the scaffolding stays an ordinary scaffolding block underneath. Ponder's vat scenes show the scaffolding turning into the stand as the vat forms.

**Mixers on vats** (Taran's design). A Mechanical Mixer used on the side of one of a vat's tanks goes on as its **vat form**: a plate of the vat's own tank, six pixels thick, standing against it, with a cog on its face in the mixer's metal, as though the mixer had been put on the inside of the vat. Since 0.23.0 it turns by that cog, from the small cogs beside it and the large cogs diagonal to it, as Create's mixer does, and no shaft goes into it (Taran; until then it was a plain plate with a shaft going into its middle). It is the same mixer used another way, so it is called after it, breaks back into it and takes its stress and speed. A mixer only fits a vat of its own material (Taran): copper, iron, steel and aluminium mixers each fit vats of their own metal (since 0.20.0; until then Create's mixer fitted wooden vats and iron and aluminium mixers had no vat form). Create's own mixer has no vat form and works only over basins, and no mixer fits a brass vat. Used on a vat it does not fit, nothing goes on and the player is told which vats it fits; sneaking puts down an ordinary mixer. Turning, each mixer stirs four batches at a time, whatever its tier, as fast as Create's mixer over a basin at that speed, so a better (faster) mixer gets through them sooner and a big vat wants several. A vat mixes only wet, unheated recipes: this mod's `mcremix:vat_mixing` and Create's own wet mixing that needs no heat. Nothing settles or ferments while its mixers turn.
- **Ferric chloride** moved here from the basin, since chlorine is a gas.
- **Ethylene dichloride**: a bucket each of chlorine and ethylene stirred over 25 mB of ferric chloride, which is used up slowly (Taran), make a bucket of it, for the Steel Furnace to crack.
- **Hydrochloric acid**: 250 mB of hydrogen chloride stirred into 250 of water make 500 mB.

**Hydrogen chloride and acid.** What the cracking gives off is either split back by an Electrolyser into chlorine and hydrogen (100 C splits 250 mB into 125 of each) or mixed into **hydrochloric acid** (Taran), which cleans rust. Poured from a bucket or an open pipe end, acid strips every weathered iron or copper block it runs onto or against back to bare metal with a fizz (waxed metal is sealed against it), then soaks away after two seconds. A Spout of it cleans a weathered part or bracket, Create's copper pipes, pumps, tanks, shingles and tiles, this mod's rusted iron pipes, pumps and tanks, copper blocks, and with Immersive Weathering its rusted iron, on a depot or belt, 100 mB each (`create:filling` recipes, one for each weathered item).

**Gases let loose** share one kind of cloud since 0.16.0, each with its own look and effect:
- **Ethylene**, a faint warm haze about as heavy as air, stays where it was let go for a few seconds and ripens every crop under it a stage (wheat and the like, stems, cocoa, sweet berries), once for each cloud (Taran).
- **Vinyl chloride**, a faint lilac haze heavier than air, sinks and hangs about as long as chlorine does. It does no harm, but anything breathing it (not the undead or golems) goes dizzy with nausea for ten seconds after its last breath of it (Taran).
- **Hydrogen chloride**, a pale mist heavier than air, rusts or weathers every iron and copper block under it a stage, parts and pipes among them, once for each mist (Taran). Waxed metal is sealed against it, and it does not reach under water.
- **Pressurised steam**, made by the Pressurizer, is a gas too, with no bucket.

**The Pressurizer** (Taran's design) is a steel machine on a shaft: a steel shaft over steel plates either side of a steel fluid tank, over steel casing. Its shaft works a piston pump, so **speed is pressure** (Taran): each job needs a speed, nothing happens below it, and twice as fast works twice as quick. It is heavy to turn, 32 su for every RPM (16,384 at 512), so it wants gearing up. Since 0.24.0 (Taran) it is two blocks: a riveted steel body with a pressure dial on its front, under a pressure vessel narrower than a block that holds what it squeezes, the two placed, broken and moved together. Since 0.26.0 (Taran) its shaft goes in at either side of the body, left or right of the dial, where the body's faces are sunk round the shaft as a chain drive's are; placed, its dial faces the player, and a shaft ahead or behind it turns it a quarter so a side meets that shaft. The vessel is square, and everything goes in and out at the vessel's four sides: fluid is piped in there and waits in a 1,000 mB tank, and what it makes comes out of the same sides, items by funnel or an empty hand (on either block), fluids by pumps. A pipe joining a side of the vessel has a nozzle stand out of it to meet the pipe. The vessel's top, where its safety valve is, and the body take nothing. The dial reads the pressure, which is speed: each mark a quarter of its top speed, the needle in the red past it, trembling while it works. A Pressurizer from an older world turns a side to its drive and gets its vessel if there is room above it; pipes and funnels on an old one's body have to move up to its vessel. Heat, for the jobs that want it, comes from the block underneath: anything that heats a Furnace from below, or a Furnace or Steel Furnace, as hot as its chamber. Goggles say what it is waiting for. It is steel, capped at steel's 512 RPM. The titanium one (Taran: steel now, titanium later; built in 0.19.1) is the same recipe in titanium round the same steel tank, takes up to 2,048 RPM, so up to four times as fast, and as a titanium machine heats as it turns, tints and burns. Its recipes (`mcremix:pressurizing`):

| Job | Speed | Heat underneath | Batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethylene into Polyethylene Pellets | 512 RPM | Warm or hotter | 100 mB, 3 s at 512 RPM |
| Vinyl chloride into PVC Resin | 256 RPM | none needed | 100 mB, 4 s at 256 RPM |
| Water into pressurised steam | 128 RPM | Hot or hotter | 20 mB a tick at 128 RPM, more the faster it turns |

**Pressurised steam in a steel boiler** (Taran) is all that runs it (since 0.26.0 water and fire do nothing for one): piped into a boiler of steel tanks, every 10 mB a tick is a boiler level, of heat and water both, up to 18. The shaft turning the Pressurizer pays for the levels.

**Polyethylene**, the second plastic: the pellets, two of them pressed over a heated basin into a **Polyethylene Bushing** (Taran), which fills the item tag `mcremix:polyethylene` that aluminium brackets and Aluminium Casing take. Power Grid's integrated circuit takes polyethylene pellets in place of its top row's outer items.

**PVC.** A press makes PVC Resin into **PVC Sheets**, and three sheets in a row make eight **PVC Pipes** (Taran). PVC pipes (Taran's own idea) are thin and plain white, 4 px thick, lying flat on the faces of blocks like Project Red's red alloy wire:
- They join along a face, round inside corners (the riser hugging the face) and over outside corners (a small bend in the air at the block's edge, with no item of its own), side-by-side runs join, and a wrench cuts a joint (and mends it). Meeting a pump, tank, basin or Create pipe, a PVC pipe rises to meet it with a white elbow to the middle of that side, and one lying on a block that holds fluid joins into it.
- They are part of Create's fluid network, one per block space, and a pump reaches twice as far through them, 32 blocks to Create's 16: the pick for long runs.
- They hold only Cold fluids (the `mcremix:fluid_ceiling/cold` tag, which counts warm and cold water as Cold): anything hotter makes them sag and burst. They never weather. Late-game lines mix PVC, steel and glass; iron and titanium make no pipes.

### Structural formulas (0.25.0)

Taran's list: the tooltip of every compound draws its structural formula, with its elements colour coded, and it shows all the time, not only with Shift (card E). Carbon is grey, hydrogen white, oxygen red, chlorine green, sodium violet, iron orange, titanium silver, aluminium pink, silicon tan, calcium pale green and phosphorus orange, as chemists colour them, lightened where they would vanish on a tooltip's dark ground. Bonds are lines (a double bond two), ions sit apart with faint lines between neighbours, and the formula is written out underneath, its subscripts, charges and counts in small pixel digits.
- **What shows one.** This mod's chemicals, as items, as fluids in JEI and as every bucket of those fluids (chlorine, hydrogen, hydrogen chloride, hydrochloric acid, salt and salt water, molten salt, caustic soda, ferric chloride, wood spirit, ethanol, ethylene, ethylene dichloride, vinyl chloride, ethylene glycol and coolant, bauxite liquor, aluminium hydroxide, alumina, rutile and titanium dioxide, titanium tetrachloride, Bakelite, polyethylene and PVC); Create's limestone; and Minecraft's water (ice, packed and blue ice, snow), sugar, quartz, clay, calcite, dripstone and bone meal.
- **How they are drawn.** Polymers show their repeat unit in brackets with an n; ionic solids show their ions (salt as a patch of its lattice, bone meal as calcium phosphate, clay as its silicate sheet with its aluminium and hydroxide ions); sugars and rings are skeletal, their carbons as corners; solutions show both parts ("NaCl in H2O", glycol and water for coolant). Steam and warm water are water.
- **Elements on their own** (Taran, 0.26.0): a material of one element shows just its symbol, in its colour, with nothing written out under it: the ingots, nuggets, sheets and blocks of iron, copper, gold, zinc, aluminium and titanium, their melts and the buckets of them, molten sodium, and diamond as carbon. Copper is drawn copper, gold gold and zinc lavender.
- **Resource packs** can change any drawing or add one for another mod's item or fluid: each is a file under `assets/<namespace>/formulas`.

## Progression outline

1. **Hearth age.** Campfires cook food (the only early cooking). The **Furnace** (crafted exactly like vanilla's, from eight cobblestone) replaces vanilla's: it has vanilla's furnace textures, but its front is cut open and it has no menu. Click things into its upper chamber and fuel into its low firebox; you see them sit on the grate, glow, and wait to be taken out. Wood fuel only reaches Warm: enough for charcoal from logs, bricks from clay, terracotta, drying sponges, cactus and sea pickle dyes.
2. **Charcoal age.** Charcoal and coal reach Hot: every vanilla smelting recipe that is not food (glass, smooth stone, copper and gold), and Coal Coke from coal. Copper unlocks copper kinetics alongside Create's andesite tier.
3. **Bloom iron.** Raw iron no longer smelts into ingots. At Searing heat (Coal Coke or blaze rods in the firebox, or soul fire or a fed blaze burner underneath) raw iron and iron ore fire into a glowing **Iron Bloom**. Strike it on an anvil four times (sparks, lava pops, a rising clang; anvils occasionally wear) or put it under Create's Mechanical Press to get an ingot and, half the time, Slag. An iron anvil costs 31 iron and the press needs an iron block, so the first blooms are struck on a **Stone Anvil** (see below). Unlocks iron kinetics, buckets, rails, etc. Create's crushing-and-washing route (9 nuggets per crushed raw iron) stays as a cheaper but leaner alternative.
4. **Steel.** Iron ingots packed in Coal Coke and sealed with clay fire at Searing into blister steel, which a Mechanical Press beats into steel ingots and sheets. Unlocks steel casing and 512 RPM parts.
5. **Casting.** A crucible over Searing heat melts copper, gold and brass, and alloys brass, to pour into sand molds or pipe through steel; over a superheated blaze burner it melts iron and makes crucible steel and netherite.
6. **Blaze & electric heat.** Create blaze burners and Power Grid heaters put furnaces into Searing or Blazing heat without fuel. Crushed raw iron fired at Blazing gives two blooms, and ancient debris only becomes netherite scrap in a Blazing furnace.
7. **Electrolysis and aluminium.** Coal tar and wood spirit drawn off the Furnace press into Bakelite, which with steel builds Power Grid's parts. An Electrolyser wired to a generator through a transformer (or several in series) splits salt water from the sea into chlorine, hydrogen and caustic soda; caustic soda dissolves bauxite washed out of red sand, the liquor settles out aluminium hydroxide, which fires into alumina, and the Electrolyser splits alumina into molten aluminium. Unlocks aluminium parts and heat sinks.
8. **Plastics.** Steel Casing turns a Furnace into a Steel Furnace, which burns fuel pumped up into it (coal tar and wood spirit from the Furnace, ethanol fermented from sugar, hydrogen from the Electrolyser) as hot as the pump's speed makes it, up to Blazing, and lit by flint and steel or a Spark Plug on a high-voltage circuit. It turns ethanol over alumina into ethylene; a steel Pressurizer geared up to 512 RPM sets ethylene into polyethylene for aluminium supports and Power Grid's integrated circuit. Chlorine and ethylene stirred in a vat over ferric chloride make ethylene dichloride, the Steel Furnace cracks it into vinyl chloride, and the Pressurizer squeezes that into PVC resin for PVC face pipes; the hydrogen chloride it gives off goes back to chlorine or into hydrochloric acid that cleans rust. Pressurised steam runs steel boilers with no fire.

### Furnace replacement summary

- **Vanilla's furnaces have gone cold.** Vanilla's furnace (renamed the **Cold Furnace**), smoker and blast furnace menus stay shut (a puff of ash and an action-bar hint point to the Furnace and campfire), and they no longer smelt even when hoppers feed them. Blocks in `mcremix:cold_furnaces`; switchable in the server config.
- Vanilla's furnace recipe makes the Furnace now, and keeps its id, so the recipe book offers it as soon as a player picks up cobblestone; nothing makes a Cold Furnace. Smokers and blast furnaces are still crafted round a furnace as in vanilla, now the Furnace (or a Cold Furnace salvaged from a ruined village), because they are butcher and armorer workstations. Cold Furnaces also still make furnace minecarts.
- **Furnace** (registered as `mcremix:kiln`, and the kiln throughout the code): all vanilla smelting (vanilla smelting recipes count as Hot) plus `mcremix:firing` recipes with explicit heat. Refuses food (food goes to campfires, or Create fan smoking in bulk). Hoppers and funnels: things to fire in through the top, fuel through the sides, results out of the bottom (the furnace also pushes them into a container below). Comparators read its heat. It burns anything that walks on it at Hot and above, and heats a Create steam boiler or basin standing on it (see Heat above). Its fuel waits until flint and steel, a fire charge or a Spark Plug lights it, and then burns until it runs out or a shovel puts it out (0.16.0).
- **Steel Furnace** (0.16.0): Steel Casing used on a Furnace. It fires the same things, burns only fluid fuel pumped up into it, hotter the faster it comes and up to Blazing, and works fluids into fluids (see Plastics, the Steel Furnace and PVC pipes above). The stone Furnace stays the early furnace and gives way to it later.
- **Coal Coke**: coal fired Hot in the furnace. Searing fuel that burns twice as long as coal.
- **Stone Anvil**: the vanilla anvil's recipe in stone (smooth stone, a stone, cobblestone), and a squat stone anvil to look at: cobblestone foot, stone waist, smooth stone face scorched where blooms land. It only strikes blooms; it has no menu, so it never stands in for a real anvil's repairs. Stone cracks: about one bloom in five chips it (a corner breaks off and a crack runs across the face), the next crack leaves it damaged, and the third crumbles it to cobblestone with a burst of stone debris, so one lasts about fifteen blooms (an iron anvil, at 3% wear per bloom, lasts around a hundred). Strikes on stone thud and throw grit as well as sparks. It stays the cheap option: three kinds of stone you can put down beside any furnace. Anvils that strike blooms are the `mcremix:bloom_anvils` block tag.
- **Slag**: bloom by-product. Fertilises like bone meal; Create washing returns iron nuggets and gravel.
- **Bellows** (Create kinetic appliance, 2 su per RPM, works at any speed): blows into the block its nozzle faces.
  - A furnace there, or a furnace standing on a fire there (campfire, lava, blaze burner...), burns **one level hotter**, up to Searing. It never reaches Blazing and never lights a furnace with no fire, and several bellows on one furnace do not stack. A furnace warmed only by an electric heater has no fire to fan and stays as it is; fuel burning in it is still fanned.
  - Fuel the bellows is fanning burns **twice as fast**, so a fanned wood fire is a Hot fire that eats wood. Fuel already as hot as fanning gets (Coal Coke, blaze rods) burns at its usual rate. A Steel Furnace is sealed, so a bellows does nothing for it.
  - A lit campfire it blows into **cooks twice as fast**.
  - Why it matters: a campfire-heated furnace reaches Hot with no fuel at all (glass, copper, coke), and a lava-heated furnace reaches Searing (iron) without coke or blaze burners. Low-tier heat stays useful.
  - Looks: the shaft turns a crank at the back whose pin rides in a yoke under the lid, so the lid pumps once per turn and squeezes a folded leather bag. Each downstroke puffs air from the copper nozzle, the fire in front leans away and flares, and a fanned furnace's firebox roars and its chimney coughs smoke. Goggles say what it is doing (fanning, no fire, already as hot as it gets, nothing in front, no furnace above this fire). Recipe: planks lid, leather around andesite alloy, planks base around a wooden (or andesite) shaft. Placed against a block it points into it; sneak to turn it round.

## Villages and villagers

- Villages are abandoned: they generate **without villagers, zombie villagers or iron golems** (this covers every structure that places them, igloo basements included). They are ruins with job-site blocks and everyday loot, not part of progression.
- **They look abandoned, too.** Every village building generates as a ruin: about a third of its cobblestone and stone bricks gone mossy (some stone bricks cracked), four windows in ten broken, every torch burnt out, campfires cold, jack o'lanterns gone dark, half the lanterns taken, half the potted flowers dead, cobwebs in the top corners of rooms, and fields run to weeds with crops at every stage of growth. The streets are dark at night, so monsters walk them. Why it matters: an empty but tidy village reads as a bug, a ruin explains itself; the ruins are also somewhere to salvage cold campfires (charcoal, or the campfire itself with Silk Touch), lanterns, beds and workstations. With Immersive Weathering installed, about one house in six burned down instead: its timber is IW's charred wood (which burns Hot in a furnace), most of its roof has fallen in, and its glass, hay, wool and flowers are gone; IW's charred blocks crumble when they lose support, so a burnt house can cave in further when disturbed. Applies to any jigsaw piece whose template sits under `village/` (vanilla's and mods' villages), after the village's own processors; houses are the pieces under a `houses/` folder. `ruinedVillages` in the server config turns it off for new chunks.
- Villagers only come from curing zombie villagers met out in the world (ordinary night spawns). Villager breeding is disabled. (Spawn eggs and commands still work for creative/admin use; golems players build still work.)
- Village chests lose progression items (diamonds, emeralds, iron and gold ingots, iron, chainmail and diamond gear, obsidian, enchanted books, saddles, horse armor); everyday items stay. The list is the item tag `mcremix:village_contraband`.

## Enchanting and XP

- **Enchanting is on hold.** A roguelike-style enchanting system is being designed separately, so phase 1 leaves vanilla enchanting alone. (An earlier "library enchanting" idea, where tables only taught enchantments from shelved books, was dropped.)
- **XP is on hold too.** XP is part of the enchanting design, so mobs, ores, smelting and trading give vanilla XP for now. (Phase 1 halved mob XP and gave XP for the first of each item obtained, by rarity; both were taken out to wait for the enchanting design.)
- Grindstone and anvil changes (experience nuggets from disenchanting, no "Too Expensive" cap) wait for the enchanting design too.

## Integrations

- **Create**: furnaces heat Create's steam boilers (registered as a `BoilerHeater`, as Power Grid registers its heaters) and basins (through a mixin on the basin's heat lookup, since a furnace's block state shows its chamber's heat, not what it gives a basin); kinetic tiers extend Create's shaft, cog, gearbox, clutch and gearshift classes, visuals and renderers; drill bits extend Create's drill (block, block entity, contraption behaviour, renderer and visuals) and give Create's own drill the iron tier; saw blades do the same for Create's saw, and the Mechanical Arm works the new saws; blooms work in the Mechanical Press; slag works in Create washing; blaze burners heat furnaces; the Bellows is a kinetic appliance (Create stress, goggles, Ponder under Kinetic Appliances); Ponder scenes for every new block; goggles show heat, fuel, oxidation, friction and limits; Create's copper shingles and tiles weather by the rules in Weathering. Since 0.16.0: the Steel Furnace takes fuel from Create's pumps and pipes, its block-state changes made without the neighbour update that would start Create's pipe flows over; the Pressurizer is a kinetic machine (Create stress, goggles, Ponder under Kinetic Appliances) that reads heat underneath as a basin does; Create's Mechanical Mixer goes on a vat's side as its vat form, and a vat runs Create's wet, unheated mixing recipes; basins let gas go; pressurised steam drives Create's steam engines through steel boilers; PVC pipes are part of Create's fluid networks, a pump reaching twice as far through them; and a Spout of hydrochloric acid cleans weathered items. Since 0.24.0 the brass, iron and steel steam engines stand in for Create's (its engine code, renderer, visual and boilers take them for its own), each kept to its own metal's boilers, and a boiler counts only the engines that run on it.
- **Create: Power Grid** (required since 0.14.0): the Electrolyser is one of its devices, a p-n junction in its circuit solver (with a steadier step limit of its own, so cells in series settle), under its Electric Devices Ponder tag; heat sinks take heat from its devices through its thermal model; its motor and transformer core take steel, its boards, switches, buttons and sockets take Bakelite, and its boards are etched in ferric chloride; heating coils and basin heaters heat furnaces by their real temperature (read at runtime); furnaces pass a heater's boiler heat up to a boiler on top; an electromagnet pulls iron out of slag (1.5 nuggets on average, twice what washing gives, but no gravel); since 0.16.0 the Spark Plug is one of its devices (a 100 kΩ gap that conducts from 900 V, under Electric Devices too), an Electrolyser splits hydrogen chloride, and its integrated circuit takes polyethylene pellets; since 0.18.0 every kinetic block keeps a temperature in its thermal model, so its thermometer, heat sinks, water coolers and fans work on aluminium supports, and a fan cools the support it blows straight onto; since 0.24.0 its electric pump comes in brass, iron, steel and aluminium, drawing as its own does, with its top speed and thermal limit scaled to each metal's pump speed. **(later)** copper kinetics conduct.
- **Immersive Weathering** (optional, looked up by id): burning furnaces slowly lay IW soot on walls above their chimney, the same way IW campfires do; iron rust uses IW's exposed / weathered / rusted names; IW's own rusting iron weathers by the rules in Weathering; IW's charred wood (left where fire passed over wood) burns Hot in a furnace, like charcoal; some ruined village houses are burnt shells of charred timber. (IW for 1.20.1 has no ash item, so wood ash is off the plan.)
- **JEI** (optional): a Furnace tab lists every firing and the non-food smelting recipes with the heat each needs, its time there and any bonus for firing hotter; a Furnace Heat tab shows, for each level, the fuels that burn that hot, the blocks that heat a furnace that much from below (lava as its bucket, fire as flint and steel, a blaze burner by how it is fed, electric heaters by temperature) and the Bellows that fans the level beneath; a Striking Blooms tab shows the anvil recipe with the stone and iron anvils as catalysts. Melting and Alloying tabs show what the crucible melts and makes, at what heat and how fast. A Settling tab shows what settles in a basin or vat, and an Electrolysis tab shows each split's charge and how long it takes at a given current, which products are gases, and that the anode burns away by power. The Furnace tab shows what a firing gives off (coal tar, wood spirit). Since 0.16.0 a Fermenting tab shows what ferments in a basin or vat, a Steel Furnace tab what it works at what heat (with any catalyst and how long it lasts), a Steel Furnace Fuel tab how many mB a tick of each fuel give each heat up to its ceiling, a Vat Mixing tab what stirs together in a vat (with the mixers that fit vats and the Oak Tank as catalysts), and a Pressurizing tab what the Pressurizer squeezes at what speed and heat; the Furnace Heat tab no longer lists hydrogen. Boiling and Arc Furnace tabs show what a heated basin boils down and what the arc melts, with the energy each takes. Since 0.25.0 (Taran's list) every one of these tabs is laid out and drawn as Create's basin pages are: inputs in Create's slot frames on the left, outputs on the right under its arrow, chance outputs in its dotted frame, the machine drawn at work in the middle over what heats it (a campfire, lava or a blaze burner) or on its shadow, and a bar along the bottom giving the heat, time, speed, charge or energy, with a slot on its end cycling through what heats it that hot and more on hovering the bar. While furnaces are cold, the furnace, smoker, blast furnace and fuel tabs are hidden, and the furnaces carry an info page saying where cooking went. Since 0.28.0 (Taran) a Casting tab shows each molten metal poured into a Sand Ingot Mold and a Sand Block Mold: the crucible tipped over a depot with the mold on it, the mold and the metal in, the casting and the sand back out, and the mold setting in two seconds.
- **WWOO**: village emptying applies to any structure that spawns vanilla villagers or zombie villagers, so modded village variants are covered; the chest filter covers loot tables under `minecraft:chests/village/` (a pack can point it at other prefixes with another loot modifier entry).
- **Better Combat**: no new weapons yet; any future weapon ships Better Combat weapon attributes.
